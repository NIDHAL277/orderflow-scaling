    Forecasting extension (F)¶

    The scaling laws say how rough volatility should be, H_σ = 2 H0 − 3/2 (paper, Remark 4.2). The forecasting extension asks the practical question: does that roughness help forecast volatility, and does the roughness implied by order flow forecast as well as the roughness measured on volatility itself? Daily traded volume gets the same treatment.

    It is a statistical evaluation (losses, Diebold–Mariano tests, model confidence set). The specification excludes trading strategies and P&L backtests (§4), and so does this extension. The design is pre-registered (config/preregistration.md §10) and frozen with the rest of config/estimation.yaml.

    ofs forecast --sample development --jobs 4      # needs the tag prereg-v1
ofs forecast --sample confirmation --jobs 4     # needs config-frozen (confirmatory)
ofs simulate forecast --reps 10 --jobs 4        # check on simulated rough volatility

    Target, data, origins¶

    • Target: the average of the series over the next h days, y_h(t) = mean(y[t+1..t+h]), h ∈ {1, 5, 22} (day, week, month; Corsi, 2009). Series: daily 5-minute realised variance (rv_5m) and daily traded quote volume (quote_volume).
    • Data: the 5-year daily history from monthly 1-minute klines (daily_stats/rv_history, ADR 0011), cut at the end of the evaluated sample: no later day is read.
    • Origins: every day of the evaluated period. development: 2023-05-01 → 2025-10-31 (the first origin needs a 500-day window); confirmation: 2025-11-01 → 2026-10-31. Forecasts use the data up to and including the origin only; a test perturbs every value after the origin and checks that no forecast moves.

    Models¶

    Model Forecast of the log series Parameters (rolling 500-day window)
    rw last value none
    ar_log AR(5) on log y, direct projection on log y_h OLS, refitted daily
    har HAR of Corsi (2009) on levels OLS, insanity filter (in-window range)
    har_log HAR on the logs of the daily, weekly, monthly averages (benchmark) OLS
    rough_gjr kernel of Gatheral, Jaisson & Rosenbaum (2018, §5) as published H by their moment regression
    rough_rolling exact predictor (below) H, noise ratio, scale by Whittle
    rough_dev exact predictor H = development estimate of H_σ; noise ratio by Whittle
    rough_theory exact predictor H = 2 Ĥ0 − 3/2 (development H0, floored at 0.01)

    Rough parameters are re-estimated every 5 days, regressions every day.

    The exact rough predictor¶

    The daily log RV is modelled as the Whittle estimator of Fukasawa, Takabatake & Westphal already models it for H_σ: the day average of a fractional Brownian motion plus white measurement noise, Y_t = c + ν · mean_{day t} W^H + ε_t. Its increments are stationary with autocovariance ν² [g_H(k) + r (2·1{k=0} − 1{|k|=1})], where r = var ε / ν² and

    g_H(k) = ½ δ⁴Φ(k),   Φ(x) = |x|^{2H+2} / ((2H+1)(2H+2))      (fourth central difference)

    is the autocovariance of the increments of day-averaged fBm (a test checks it against the spectral density the Whittle estimator uses, to 6 digits). The forecast of Y_{t+D} is the Gaussian conditional expectation given the last 499 increments: one Toeplitz system per horizon (Levinson recursion), weights on past values summing to one (no level parameter). The same algebra gives the prediction-error variance, hence the level forecast E[e^{Y_{t+D}}] = exp(forecast + ½ var). A test checks on simulated paths that the one-step mean squared error equals that variance.

    Why not just the published kernel¶

    The GJR (2018) formula is the prediction of a continuously observed, noise-free fBm with an infinite past. Daily RV is neither: it is a daily average and it carries estimation noise. In a probe on simulated fBm sampled daily (H = 0.1, 200-day window), the one-step mean squared error of the kernel discretised by cell integrals was 13 % above that of the exact Gaussian predictor (midpoint cells: 5 %); the kernel puts 63 % of the weight on the last day. With measurement noise the gap widens. rough_gjr is kept as the published benchmark, rough_rolling is the principal rough model.

    From log forecasts to levels¶

    Every log model maps its forecast to a level with its own lognormal correction exp(μ + s²/2): in-window residual variance (regressions), model-implied conditional variance (exact predictor), c_H K D^{2H} with c_H = Γ(3/2−H) / (Γ(H+1/2) Γ(2−2H)) (GJR kernel). The empirical smearing factor of Duan (1983) is available (level_correction: smearing) but is noisy at long horizons: 250 overlapping 22-day ratios hold about 11 independent blocks. With the true parameters, smearing cost the exact predictor about 10 % of QLIKE at 22 days in the probe, the lognormal correction 0.5 %.

    Evaluation¶

    • Losses on the level of the target: QLIKE y/f − log(y/f) − 1 (primary) and squared error. With a noisy but conditionally unbiased proxy (daily RV for integrated variance) both rank forecasts as the latent variance would (Patton, 2011); the squared error of logs does not and is not used.
    • Per pair: mean losses on the dates where every model forecasts, ratio to har_log, Diebold–Mariano test against har_log (Newey–West with max(h−1, ⌊4(T/100)^{2/9}⌋) lags, Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold correction, Student t), model confidence set of Hansen, Lunde & Nason (2011) at 90 % (T_max statistic, stationary bootstrap of dates, mean block 22 days).
    • Pooled across pairs (hypotheses): per pair the loss differential is scaled by the mean loss of the reference model, averaged across the pairs present each date, and the DM test is run on that single series (cross-sectional correlation between cryptos is then harmless). Its mean is the average relative change of the loss, ratio − 1, with a stationary-bootstrap 95 % CI over dates.
    • Decision rule: the one of the main tests, on the relative loss change, with margin 0.02: better / worse when the Holm-corrected DM test rejects at 1 % and |ratio − 1| > 0.02; equivalent when it does not reject and the CI lies within [0.98, 1.02]; undecided otherwise.
    Hypothesis Target Model / reference Reading
    F1 volatility rough_rolling / har_log does roughness forecast better than HAR?
    F2 volatility rough_theory / rough_dev does the order-flow H forecast as well as the volatility H? The theory passes if equivalent
    F3 volume rough_rolling / har_log F1 for traded volume

    On the development sample rough_dev and rough_theory take H from estimates of the same period: their rows are flagged look_ahead and are exploratory. On the confirmation sample H comes from the development sample only, so F2 is a genuine out-of-sample test of the scaling law.

    Simulation check¶

    ofs simulate forecast simulates the model of the exact predictor (H ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}, noise sd ∈ {0, 0.3}, ν = 0.3, 1,500 days) and reports each model's QLIKE relative to har_log (data/simulation/forecast_study.parquet, report table tab:forecast-sim).

    Full run (2026-11-10, 10 replications per cell; mean ratio to har_log, with the range over the six (H, noise) cells):

    Model h = 1 h = 5 h = 22
    rough_rolling (H by Whittle on the window) 0.988-0.992 0.960-0.979 0.873-0.932
    rough_dev (true H) 0.986-0.989 0.946-0.974 0.860-0.927
    rough_gjr (published kernel) 0.995-1.083 0.983-1.083 0.901-0.999
    ar_log 1.003-1.015 0.996-1.030 0.968-1.037
    har (levels) 1.008-1.609 1.015-2.307 1.015-1.680
    rw 1.08-1.61 1.27-2.65 1.55-3.87

    The exact predictor beats HAR in 90-100 % of the replications at one day and in 70-90 % at 22 days; the Whittle H of the window is close to the truth (0.105 for 0.1, 0.215 for 0.2; 0.10 for 0.05 with noise, where roughness and noise are hardest to separate). The published kernel loses to HAR at short horizons once there is measurement noise (+8 % at H = 0.05) but helps at 22 days. HAR on levels is unstable at H = 0.2 (ratios up to 2.3 with a large spread): heavy-tailed levels defeat the insanity filter, which is why the logs version is the benchmark.

    Earlier exploratory run (4 replications, H = 0.1, noise 0.3):

    Model h = 1 h = 5 h = 22
    rough_rolling 0.989 0.976 0.915
    rough_dev (true H) 0.988 0.975 0.915
    rough_gjr 1.030 1.053 0.981
    ar_log 1.019 1.055 1.084
    har (levels) 1.028 1.055 1.053
    rw 1.40 2.10 2.88

    Under rough volatility the exact predictor wins and its advantage grows with the horizon; estimating H costs nothing against knowing it (Whittle underestimates H and the noise ratio together on 500-day windows, with little effect on the forecasts). A weekly CI test (tests/simulation/test_forecast_sim.py, marked slow) keeps these orderings.

    Live forecasts (production)¶

    The daily run (ofs daily) also forecasts every pair at the end of the day just completed, with every configured model, for the next 1, 5 and 22 days (ofs.forecast.live). The series is the 5-year history (complete months, klines) followed by the daily aggTrades statistics for the days it does not cover yet: the same 5-minute RV, and the notional traded as the quote volume. Each day's forecasts are written once (data/forecast/live/{day}.parquet) and never recomputed; as targets complete, the run scores them (live_scored.parquet), which builds a live, genuinely out-of-sample track record of every model. GET /forecast/live serves the latest forecasts (with the annualised volatility sqrt(365 × average daily variance)); the dashboard shows them as a "live outlook" with the track record. Live forecasts estimate on real data, so they start once the protocol allows the rolling estimates (tag prereg-v1).

    Outputs¶

    data/forecast/: forecasts_{sample}.parquet (one row per pair, market, target, model, horizon and origin, with the realised target, both losses, H and the noise ratio), evaluation_{sample}.parquet (per pair) and tests_{sample}.parquet (F1–F3). They are served by the API (/forecast/tests, /forecast/evaluation, /forecasts/{symbol}, /forecast/live), shown on the dashboard page Forecasting, and in the report (table tab:forecast, figure 9).

    Limitations¶

    • The rough predictor assumes the H of the window holds over the horizon; regime changes are not modelled by the forecasts (the change-point monitor of H0, changepoints, flags them separately).
    • No weekday effect is modelled for any model (weekends trade less in crypto).
    • rough_theory floors 2 Ĥ0 − 3/2 at 0.01: for Ĥ0 < 0.755 the theory predicts a non-positive H_σ, which no fBm predictor can use; the number of floored pairs is reported.
